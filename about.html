ABOUT

About Me

Plant pathologist developing research expertise in plant genetics, genomics, molecular breeding and host resistance.

My Research Journey

I am a graduate researcher in Plant Pathology at North Dakota State University, where my research focuses on understanding genetic resistance to plant-parasitic nematodes in soybean and dry bean.

My research background began in plant pathology and disease-resistance phenotyping, but my graduate research has increasingly drawn me toward plant genetics, quantitative genomics, and molecular breeding. Working with soybean germplasm, molecular markers, and genome-wide association analysis has made me especially interested in understanding how genetic variation contributes to complex agronomic traits.

Moving forward, I hope to deepen my training in quantitative genetics, statistical genomics, computational genomics, and genomic prediction. I am particularly interested in integrating phenotypic and genomic data to understand complex trait architecture and translate genetic discoveries into practical crop improvement.

Current Research Direction

My current work combines controlled-environment and field phenotyping with molecular approaches to characterize genetic resistance in soybean and dry bean.

I am particularly interested in connecting resistance phenotypes with underlying genetic mechanisms and exploring how genomic and computational tools can improve our understanding of crop resistance.

Future Interests

My long-term research interests include plant genetics and genomics, molecular mechanisms of host resistance, crop improvement, molecular breeding, host–pathogen interactions and computational biology.
